China Just Added A New Entrance Exam For Foreign Students

It's called the CSCA. It started four months ago. If you're applying to a Chinese undergraduate program for this cycle, there's a real chance you haven't heard of it yet, and that's exactly the problem.

Cipher Educational Consult · September 2026 · 9 min read

In this guide

1. What CSCA actually is 2. Who actually has to take it 3. What's actually tested 4. Dates, format, and fees 5. How to register, and the scam risk 6. How this fits with everything else

We wrote a longer piece a few weeks ago on how scholarships actually work, and mentioned this exam briefly as the current example of how fast these systems change. It deserves its own explanation, because it's new enough that a lot of what's online about applying to Chinese universities right now simply doesn't mention it.

1. What CSCA actually is

CSCA stands for China Scholastic Competency Assessment. It's a standardized test, run by the China Scholarship Council together with subject experts from Chinese universities, built specifically for international students applying to undergraduate degrees in China.

It's brand new. China formally established it between August and September 2025, the first exam sat on December 21, 2025, and starting with the 2026/2027 intake, it became a required part of the undergraduate application at Chinese Government Scholarship universities.

Think of it as similar in purpose to the SAT for US undergraduate admissions, a single standardized measure universities can use to compare applicants coming from completely different school systems, whether you finished WAEC, A-Levels, or the IB, on the same scale.

2. Who actually has to take it

Undergraduate applicants only. If you're applying for a Master's or a PhD, this doesn't apply to you at all, that surprises a lot of people who assume a new "entrance exam" means everyone.

Right now, it's confirmed mandatory for anyone applying to an undergraduate program at a Chinese Government Scholarship university. China's own stated plan is to expand it to every international undergraduate applicant nationwide, scholarship or not, by 2028. Some universities are already requiring it ahead of that date for their own admissions, not just scholarship applicants.

Because individual universities can move faster than the national timeline, the only way to know for certain is to check directly with your specific target university before you assume you're exempt.

3. What's actually tested

  • Mathematics, compulsory for every applicant, no exceptions.
  • Professional Chinese, required if your program is taught in Chinese.
  • Physics and Chemistry, required depending on your intended major, an engineering applicant will face different subject requirements than a humanities applicant.

Each subject can be taken in Chinese or English, you choose the language your target university requires. And here's the part that should ease some nerves: there's no pass or fail, and no minimum score set by CSCA itself. It produces a score report, and each university sets its own cutoff for that score, independently, based on its own admission standards. It's measuring whether you can academically keep up, not judging your past grades.

4. Dates, format, and fees

Starting in 2026, CSCA runs five times a year: January, March, April, June, and December. A few confirmed sessions to anchor your planning against:

SessionRegistrationResults
Dec 21, 2025 (special)Nov 1–30, 2025Jan 7, 2026
Mar 15, 2026 (most used for Fall 2026)Jan 15–Feb 28, 2026Apr 1, 2026
Dec 10, 2026 (final 2026 sitting)Oct 1–Nov 20, 2026Jan 5, 2027

Exams are mostly home-based, computer-based, and remotely proctored by webcam and microphone, with a limited number of physical test centers gradually being added, seats at physical centers are limited and go on a first-come, first-served basis. Results are typically released within about two weeks of sitting.

The exam fee is ¥450 for one subject, or ¥700 for two or more, payable by Alipay, WeChat Pay, or bank transfer. Worth flagging early: none of those three payment methods are straightforward from a Nigerian bank card without some setup, that's a real, practical hurdle, not a small one.

5. How to register, and the scam risk

You create an account and register directly through the official CSCA platform. Because this system is brand new, it's also exactly the kind of gap where lookalike websites and "guaranteed registration" scams show up fastest, nobody's built up years of familiarity with what the real portal looks like yet.

Before you register or pay anyone anything, confirm the current official CSCA registration link directly through your target university's international admissions office, or through the Chinese embassy's own published notice. Don't trust a link forwarded in a WhatsApp group, no matter how official it looks.

6. How this fits with everything else

CSCA doesn't replace anything you've already heard about, it sits alongside it. HSK is still the separate Chinese-language requirement for Chinese-taught programs. Your GPA and study plan still matter for the scholarship evaluation itself. CSCA is a new, additional academic gate, specific to undergraduate applicants, that simply didn't exist a year ago.

If you haven't already, the scholarships guide covers how those other pieces, HSK, GPA thresholds, the Type A and Type B application channels, actually fit together.

This is exactly why "what I read last year" isn't good enough anymore. The rules changed in December. If your plan was built before that, it's time to check it again.
